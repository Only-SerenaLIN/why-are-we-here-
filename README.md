# why-are-we-here-
This is my first reflection for SD5913.
I am taking SD5913 because I want to understand how programming can become part of my design process, rather than treating code as something separate from creative work.

My undergraduate background is in art education. Unlike most of my classmates, I don't have a design background. I'm not good at playing games either. I only have years of training in "seeing", observing composition, color, and why a picture is good. And I've been trained to teach others to see it the same way. This year, machines can already write code in one sentence. So why am I still learning here? I don't intend to speak well of this course. I just want to answer for myself. In 2026, code is becoming one of the materials for image creation, and code is increasingly capable of writing itself. If I only know how to use the tools built on top of the code, I will never know where their boundaries lie. Technology is never neutral; it shapes what you think is "possible". The medium I can't understand is like a wall I can't see. Artists understand their materials, painters understand pigments, and photographers understand light. This is not mysticism; it's about knowing what you can do and what a dead end is before you waste a week. So I think I need to understand it. Machines write code, and someone has to know whether it's written well or not. For others, this is a niche skill, but for me, it's a job responsibility. If I were to be a design teacher in the future. My students grew up in an AI environment and they will bring those pictures into the classroom. If I treat the machine as a black box, I won't be able to teach it; I can only demonstrate. Reading code, even if it's done slowly, turns the black box into a tool with boundaries. This is the difference between "explaining a painting" and "pointing to a painting".

Furthermore, from a historical perspective, photography once claimed to bring an end to painting, and synthesizers once claimed to bring an end to music. In 1965, Nake and Nees were even accused of "creating machines to replace artists" when they used computers to paint. None of the results happened. In my opinion, the medium has changed. People who understand new media have gained new ways of playing and will have greater power and more room for operation. This weekend, I went to the award ceremony of the undergraduate graduation project competition. Someone took the main visual poster I made and directly used AI to dynamically transform it into a stage background video without asking me. This is an act of infringement. But what really hurt me was not the anger, but that if I had known how to use AI and write code, I could have turned that flat poster into something interactive, and they wouldn't have misused it and made it so ugly.

Actually, this course is very difficult for me. I need to make up for it bit by bit in the evening. But this discomfort itself is evidence that everything worth learning is not natural at the very beginning. Why are we here? I want to know what the role of programming is? How exactly did he develop a software and application step by step? What was his train of thought? All of these make me very curious.
